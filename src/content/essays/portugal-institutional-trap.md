Every Portuguese productivity initiative I have read since the EU accession in 1986 reaches for the same lever. The lever is some version of *make capital cheaper, make taxes lower, make the bureaucracy faster*. The lever does not move. The initiative ends. A new one begins under a new government with a different logo. The lever does not move again.

The reason is not the lever. The reason is what the lever is connected to.

## The trap, named

Portuguese institutions are organised around *risk avoidance for the institution*, not outcome production for the citizen. This sounds abstract. It is operationally concrete. Look at any decision a Portuguese institution makes, ask "who carries the cost if this goes wrong?", and you will find that the answer is almost always *the official who decided*. The official's incentive is therefore to not decide, or to decide in the way that has been decided before, or to require so much paperwork that the decision is technically the citizen's.

Once you see this, you cannot unsee it. The slow planning permission is not slow because of paperwork. It is slow because nobody wants to be the official who approved a project that later embarrasses them. The slow procurement is not slow because of regulation. It is slow because the regulation is the cover for the absence of judgement.

The lever is on the wrong end.

## Why tax policy doesn't move it

When you cut taxes inside a system organised around institutional risk avoidance, you do not unlock production. You unlock more institutions, because what gets created with the freed capital is *another institution risk-averse enough to defend itself*. Portugal has invented an extraordinary number of public-private hybrids in the last twenty years for exactly this reason. They are not businesses. They are institutional risk distributors.

This is also why simplification programmes fail. You cannot simplify a system whose complexity is the load-bearing wall. The complexity is what allows the official to defer the decision. Take it away and the official cannot function, because the official was never trained to make the decision.

## What would actually work

The thing that would move productivity in Portugal is not a tax cut. It is *changing who carries the cost of a wrong decision*. Pull the cost up to the institution. Make the institution carry it instead of the individual official. You will get more decisions, faster decisions, and many more wrong ones. That last part is the price of the first two. Most Portuguese reform programmes are unwilling to name that price, because the price is short-term embarrassment, and embarrassment is the currency of Portuguese institutional politics.

This is also why I think agentic AI is interesting for Portuguese institutions in a way it is not interesting for, say, American ones. American institutions already make decisions and absorb the consequences. The bottleneck is volume. A Portuguese institution does not have a volume problem. It has a *who-decided* problem. An agent that takes a decision is not faster than the official. It is *deciding for the institution rather than the official*. That is the actual unlock.

## The shorter version

The lever the reform programmes have been pulling for forty years is connected to the wrong machine. The machine that needs the lever is the one that decides who carries the cost of a wrong decision. Until that machine moves, capital will keep arriving and decisions will keep not being made.

I have been turning this over for a year. I do not have a clean answer for what the new lever looks like. I have a clear answer for which lever is wrong.
